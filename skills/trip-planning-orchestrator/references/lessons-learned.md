# Lessons Learned And Corrections

## Planning And Conversation

- Do not optimize one traveler's flight while ignoring companions from another origin. Compare the shared destination using both prices and schedules.
- Cheap flights can still produce a poor trip when arrival is late, departure is early, or a transfer consumes the only useful day.
- Do not re-ask confirmed preferences. Maintain a decision log and say which later days a new choice changes.
- Keep transfer legs visible. A day that shows only sights hides the real fatigue and makes day numbering appear broken.
- For peak holidays, separate net driving time from a conservative execution budget and add explicit cutoff rules.
- Do not stack a full scenic-area visit onto a long mountain transfer merely because a tour itinerary does it. Package tours, self-drive, and private-driver trips have different tolerances.
- Do not update maps and posters while the route is still being negotiated. Agree on the text, then update every derivative together.

## Live Research

- If the user requires an answer based on specific articles, read those articles in the available browser. If access fails, explain the exact limitation and keep trying appropriate surfaces; never improvise article content.
- Search snippets and AI summaries are not proof. Open the supporting page.
- Flight and hotel prices are snapshots. Include search time and conditions.
- A hotel result can mean sold out, inventory not released, or login-only price. Keep those states separate.
- Cancellation policy belongs beside each chosen hotel and in the reservation TODOs.
- For tickets or resident discounts, record both eligibility and whether free admission still requires advance reservation.

## Maps And Posters

- A map-looking illustration is not geographically reliable unless points come from coordinates and routes come from roads/transit.
- Never let labels cover route strokes merely to keep them near the point. Move the label outward and use a leader line.
- Fixing one overlap can create another. Reinspect the entire route, legend, inset maps, day chips, stays, and page edges after each layout change.
- Numbered markers and white information boxes should share the same number so readers can match them quickly.
- Use icons as secondary cues. The place name must still fit; long labels need width or wrapping.
- Alternative routes should be dashed, mutually exclusive when appropriate, and labeled at their endpoints. A legend alone is insufficient when branches are dense.
- Insets should show ordered entry and movement, such as scenic entrance -> shuttle -> first stop, not just a list of attractions.
- Crop empty map regions and enlarge useful detail instead of filling space with explanatory text.
- Export posters from a fixed canvas at high device scale. Browser full-page stitching can repeat or blur long layouts.

## PDF And Document Export

- Mixed portrait/landscape CSS can leave a heading-only transition page. Test page count and page dimensions, then render the actual PDF before delivery.
- Do not judge PDF quality from the HTML preview. Inspect rasterized pages, especially route maps, summary tables, dense alternatives, and the last page.
- A high-resolution source image can still look small if placed conservatively. Balance physical page size, margins, and readable text rather than only increasing pixels.

## Safety And Scope

- Weather-dependent mountain branches must have a morning decision point, route-specific lodging consequence, and a safe abandonment rule.
- Do not promise access to informal roads or viewpoints based on a single community post. Verify near departure and defer to the driver/operator on the day.
- Package reusable process, schemas, and checklists, not personal bookings, browser profiles, cookies, or another skill's proprietary implementation.
